Structure

Each presentation has to answer the following fundamental questions:
• what is this about

• why is this important
• what are the results

• what is the takeaway
Typically people remember the first and the last slide, so these questions
should be answered there. The body of the presentation carries you from
the first 2 questions to the last two.
Imagine your audience is very tired: they will only really pay attention to
the first and the last slide.

Readability matters

Never put too much on one slide; use bullets.
• Make a roadmap slide

• Fonts should be large and legible
• Use one style throughout

• Colors should be contrasting (never use pale ones)
• Never put large chunks of text

• Make sure figures have good resolution

• Do not get carried away with fancy slides

Poor practices

• Reading the slide word by word

• Saying “here is a bunch of graphs”. . . and showing graphs
• Showing a correlation matrix with 120 features

What you say and what you write on the slide should be diﬀerent things.
Compress the written message, for example:
What you say: We use regularization with out-of-sample chosen
parameters to get a better fit than vanilla OLS, but the random forest
with default parameters performs even better, presumably due to
non-linearities.
What you show: OLS loss: 2.36, Elastic Net loss: 2.18, RF loss: 2.11

Slide-by-slide

• Presentation name and team members
• Intro: why is this important

• Intro: summarize what was done before you
• Roadmap

• (1-2 slides) Data: columns, missing values
• (2-3 slides) Visual: pairplots, logarithms
• (1-2 slides) Few baseline models

• (2-3 slides) More complex models

• (1-2 slides) Experiment with design (stacking? cv...)
• Summary and comparison of the results
• Conclusion

Final tips

• Watch the other groups’ presentations, identify strengths and weaknesses
• Practice your presentation at least 2 times
• Leave at least a day to polish
• Split the workload

• Do teamwork, but choose 1 leader
